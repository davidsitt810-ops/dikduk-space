Two numbers per word (0–1): your own consonants found where the word belongs, and the hazzan's sounds for the word found in your take; the third is the Hebrew-letter head's score. Red = both near zero (the word was not there), or — on a clean take — the combined score (consonants, letters heard, vowels) is sure the word was not sung. Yellow = not sure. "Not heard" names the letters the head did not find. A one-consonant word is never marked red on its own. Your scorecard calibrates this.

Throat letters ע ח

Three ears vote on each ע and ח: a Hebrew-letter head trained on the archive's hazzanim, a classifier on the Arabic model's features (taught the plain sounds from Israeli speech), and the Arabic-letter ear that writes ع / ح when it hears the throat. "From the throat" needs two votes and none against; "not sure" is said as such. The throat drill below is what sharpens them for your voice.

Vowels

A new ear, trained on Rabbi Alouf's pointed Tehillim: it reads which vowel class you sang after each letter and compares it with the hazzan's (and with the nikud, for Tehillim). Only clear disagreements are listed; a word with a different number of vowels is left alone. This does not change a word's ✓/✕ yet — it is being calibrated on your takes.

Throat drill

Short recordings that teach the checker your throat ע and ח and the plain ones. Say (or sing) the word alone, about 2 seconds. Each word twice: once from the throat, once the plain way (ע like א, ח like כ). Each one is kept on the server with its features; the verdict shown is the machine's current guess, so you can see where it is wrong.
